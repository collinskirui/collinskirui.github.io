Law of Large Numbers

Law of Large Numbers (LLN) is a fundamental principle in probability theory stating that as a random experiment is repeated many times, the average of outcomes converges to the expected value.

For repeated, independent trials, the empirical mean (or relative frequency) stabilizes near the theoretical expectation as the number of trials increases.

Approach

Let X₁, X₂, …, Xₙ be independent and identically distributed random variables with expectation E[X]. The sample mean is:

$$f(n) = \frac{X_1 + X_2 + \cdots + X_n}{n}$$

According to LLN: $$f(n) \to E[X] \quad \text{as } n \to \infty$$

We model Xᵢ as Bernoulli trials (success = 1, failure = 0) with success probability p = 0.3. Hence E[X] = p. As n grows, the relative frequency of successes converges to 0.3.

Interpretation

  • At the beginning, f(n) fluctuates randomly.
  • As trials increase, fluctuations decrease, and all trajectories cluster near p = 0.3.
  • This illustrates the essence of the Law of Large Numbers.

Conclusion

  • Random results become predictable in their average.
  • Sample mean stabilizes near true probability p.
  • Variability decreases as n increases.
