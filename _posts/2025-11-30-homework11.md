# 1.  Introduction
The **Wiener process**, also called [**standard Brownian motion**](https://hpaulkeeler.com/wiener-or-brownian-motion-process/) is a continuous-time stochastic process that displays random continuous “wiggly’’ behaviour. It is the mathematical model behind physical Brownian motion, diffusion, heat flow, stock-price modeling, and is the cornerstone of stochastic calculus.

In discrete form, one may think of Brownian motion as the limit of a symmetric random walk when the number of steps goes to infinity and the step size shrinks appropriately. This connection is formalized by the **Donsker invariance principle**, but here we focus on simulation and observable properties.

---

# 2.  Formal Definition

A stochastic process $$\{W(t),\, t \ge 0\}$$ is a **standard Wiener process** if:

1. **Initial condition:**  
   $$W(0) = 0$$

2. **Independent increments:**  
   For any ordered times  
   $$0 \le t_1 < t_2 < \cdots < t_n,$$  
   the increments $$W(t_k) - W(t_{k-1})$$ are independent.

3. **Gaussian increments:**  
   For all $$s > 0,$$  
   $$W(t+s) - W(t) \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\, s)$$

4. **Continuous paths:**  
   With probability 1, $$t \mapsto W(t)$$ is continuous.

From these axioms:

i. $$\mathbb{E}[W(t)] = 0$$  
ii. $$\operatorname{Var}[W(t)] = t$$  
iii. $$W(t) \sim \mathcal{N}(0,t)$$  

The corresponding transition density is:

$$
f_{W(t)}(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}
\exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{2t}\right)
$$

---

# 3. Key Properties
---

## 3.1 Stationary and Gaussian increments

If $$\Delta W = W(t+h) - W(t),$$ then:

$$
\Delta W \sim \mathcal{N}(0, h)
$$

independent of the starting time.

---

## 3.2 Scaling property

For any constant $$c > 0$$:

$$
W(ct) \stackrel{d}{=} \sqrt{c}\, W(t).
$$

---

## 3.3  Continuity but nowhere differentiability

Sample paths are continuous everywhere but almost surely nowhere differentiable — they oscillate too wildly.

---

## 3.4 Martingale property

$$
\mathbb{E}[W(t)\mid\mathcal{F}_s] = W(s) \quad (s < t)
$$

---

## 3.5 Markov property

$$
\mathbb{P}(W(t+h)\in A \mid \mathcal{F}_t)
=
\mathbb{P}(W(t+h)\in A \mid W(t)).
$$

---

# 4. Simulation via Discretization

Let $$[0,1]$$ be divided into $$N$$ steps:

$$
\Delta t = \frac{1}{N}.
$$

Brownian increments:

$$
\Delta W_i = \sqrt{\Delta t}\, Z_i, \qquad Z_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1).
$$

Path update:

$$
W_{i+1} = W_i + \Delta W_i.
$$

As $$N \to \infty$$ this converges to true Brownian motion.

# 6. Simulating Brownian Motion Using Euler–Maruyama

The implementation of the **Euler–Maruyama (EM)** method for general SDEs:

$$
dX_t = a(X_t,t)\,dt + b(X_t,t)\,dW_t.
$$

![Euler–Maruyama](/assets/img/posts/2024-10-12-Datasets and Distrubtion/histogram euler_bm.png)

For Brownian motion:

- Drift: $$a(x,t)=0$$  
- Diffusion: $$b(x,t)=1$$  

Thus the EM update becomes:

$$
W_{n+1} = W_n + \sqrt{\Delta t}\, Z_n, \qquad Z_n \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1).
$$

which exactly matches the discretization used earlier.
