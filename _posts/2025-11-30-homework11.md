# 1.  Introduction
The **Wiener process**, also called [**standard Brownian motion**](https://hpaulkeeler.com/wiener-or-brownian-motion-process/) is a continuous-time stochastic process that displays random continuous “wiggly’’ behaviour. It is the mathematical model behind physical Brownian motion, diffusion, heat flow, stock-price modeling, and is the cornerstone of stochastic calculus.

In discrete form, one may think of Brownian motion as the limit of a symmetric random walk when the number of steps goes to infinity and the step size shrinks appropriately. This connection is formalized by the **Donsker invariance principle**, but here we focus on simulation and observable properties.

---

# 2.  Formal Definition

A stochastic process $$\{W(t),\, t \ge 0\}$$ is a **standard Wiener process** if:

1. **Initial condition:**
$$ W(0) = 0 $$

2. **Independent increments:**  
For any ordered times  $$0 \le t_1 < t_2 < \cdots < t_n,$$ the increments   $$ W(t_k) - W(t_{k-1})
$$ are independent.

3. **Gaussian increments:**  
   For all $$s > 0,$$ $$ W(t+s) - W(t) \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\, s) $$

4. **Continuous paths:**  
   With probability 1, the sample path $$t \mapsto W(t)$$ is continuous.

From these axioms:

i.   $$ \mathbb{E}[W(t)] = 0 $$

ii.  $$ \operatorname{Var}[W(t)] = t $$

iii. $$ W(t) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t) $$
- This can be referred to **martingale**
- Therefore the process **Markov** with transition density  

  $$
  f_{W(t)}(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}
  \exp\!\left(-\frac{x^2}{2t}\right)
  $$

- These properties are used widely in probability, mathematical finance and physics.

# 3. Key Properties
---
## 3.1 Stationary and Gaussian increments

Increments depend only on the time difference. If

$$
\Delta W = W(t+h) - W(t),
$$

then

$$
\Delta W \sim \mathcal{N}(0, h),
$$

regardless of the starting time $$t$$.

---

## 3.2 Scaling property

For any constant $$c > 0$$,

$$
W(ct) \stackrel{d}{=} \sqrt{c}\,W(t).
$$

This scaling property produces Brownian motion’s fractal roughness.

---

## 3.3  Continuity but nowhere differentiability

Brownian sample paths are continuous everywhere, but almost surely differentiable nowhere.  

---

## 3.4  Martingale property

Conditional expectations satisfy:

$$
\mathbb{E}[W(t)\mid\mathcal{F}_s] = W(s) \quad \text{for } s < t.
$$

---

## 3.5  Markov property

The future depends only on the present:

$$
\mathbb{P}\big(W(t+h)\in A \mid \mathcal{F}_t\big)
=
\mathbb{P}\big(W(t+h)\in A \mid W(t)\big).
$$

---

# 4. Simulation via Discretization

To simulate Brownian motion numerically, we divide the interval $$[0,1]$$ into $$N$$ equal steps:

$$
\Delta t = \frac{1}{N}.
$$

For each step:

$$
\Delta W_i = \sqrt{\Delta t}\, Z_i,
\qquad
Z_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0,1).
$$

The discrete approximation to the Wiener path is:

$$
W_{i+1} = W_i + \Delta W_i.
$$

As $$N \to \infty,$$ this converges to true Brownian motion.
