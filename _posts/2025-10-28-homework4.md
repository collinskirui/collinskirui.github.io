[Law of Large Numbers (LLN)](https://www.probabilitycourse.com/chapter7/7_1_1_law_of_large_numbers.php) is a fundamental principle in probability theory that asserts that as a random experiment is repeated numerous times, the average of the outcomes will converge towards the expected value or true predicted average of those outcomes.

```For repeated, independent trials, the empirical mean (or relative frequency) stabilizes near the theoretical expectation as the number of trials increases.```


## Approach

Let X₁, X₂, …, Xₙ be independent and identically distributed random variables with expectation E[X].  
The sample mean is defined as:

$$
f(n) = \frac{X_1 + X_2 + \cdots + X_n}{n}
$$

According to the Law of Large Numbers:

$$
f(n) \to E[X] \quad \text{as } n \to \infty
$$

In this case, we model Xᵢ as Bernoulli trials — i.e., success = 1, failure = 0 — with success probability p = 0.3.  
Hence E[X] = p.  
As n grows, the relative frequency of successes (our running mean) converges toward 0.3.

### Interpretation

- At the beginning of each trajectory, f(n) fluctuates randomly.  
- As the number of trials increases, these fluctuations decrease, and all trajectories cluster close to the expected value (p = 0.3).  

This illustrate the essence of the **Law of Large Numbers**: randomness cancels out over many repetitions.

---

## Conclusion

The simulation and visualizations show that with many independent trials:

- Random results become predictable in their average.  
- The sample mean stabilizes near the true probability \(p\).  
- Variability decreases as \(n\) increases.

Thus, the **Law of Large Numbers** guarantees that empirical averages are trustworthy estimates of theoretical expectations in the long run.
