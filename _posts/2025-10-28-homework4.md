[Law of Large Numbers (LLN)](https://www.probabilitycourse.com/chapter7/7_1_1_law_of_large_numbers.php) is a fundamental principle in probability theory: as a random experiment is repeated many times, the average outcome converges to the expected value.

```For repeated, independent trials, the empirical mean stabilizes near the theoretical expectation as the number of trials increases.```

## Approach

Let X₁, X₂, …, Xₙ be independent, identically distributed random variables with expectation E[X].  
The sample mean:

$$
f(n) = \frac{X_1 + X_2 + \cdots + X_n}{n}
$$

According to the Law of Large Numbers:

$$
f(n) \to E[X] \quad \text{as } n \to \infty
$$

Model Xᵢ as Bernoulli trials (success=1, failure=0) with p=0.3.  
Hence E[X] = p. As n increases, the running mean converges to 0.3.

### Interpretation

- Early in each trajectory, f(n) fluctuates randomly.  
- As trials increase, fluctuations decrease; trajectories cluster around p=0.3.  
- This illustrates **Law of Large Numbers**: randomness cancels out over many repetitions.

---

### Conclusion

- Random results become predictable on average.  
- Sample mean stabilizes near true probability p.  
- Variability decreases as n increases.
