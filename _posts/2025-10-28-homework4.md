[Law of Large Numbers (LLN)](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/data-science/what-is-dataset/) is a structured collection of related data, usually organized in rows and columns, representing information about a specific category or domain.

For repeated, independent trials, the empirical mean (or relative frequency) stabilizes near the theoretical expectation as the number of trials increases.


## Approach
Let X1,X2,…,Xn be independent and identically distributed random variables with expectation E[X].
The sample mean after n trials is:
f(n)=frac{X_1 + X_2 + \... + X_n}{n}f(n)=nX1+X2+⋯+Xn 
The Law of Large Numbers states that:
f(n)→E[X]asn→∞f(n) \to E[X] \quad \text{as} \quad n \to \inftyf(n)→E[X]asn→∞ 
In this demo, we use a Bernoulli process (success = 1, failure = 0) with success probability ppp.
Hence E[X]=pE[X] = pE[X]=p.
As n grows, the relative frequency of successes stabilizes near p.

## Histogram Representation

To better visualize stability after many repetitions, we can run the experiment multiple times and plot the distribution of final sample means.
When the number of trials is large, these means will cluster tightly around the expected value  𝑝=0.6.

<script>
let finalMeans = [];
for (let run = 0; run < 1000; run++) {
  let trials = Array.from({length: n}, () => Math.random() < p ? 1 : 0);
  let mean = trials.reduce((a,b)=>a+b)/n;

### Interpretation
At the beginning of each trajectory, f(n) fluctuates randomly.
As the number of trials increases, these fluctuations decrease, and all trajectories cluster close to the expected value p=0.6.
This illustrates the Law of Large Numbers: empirical averages become reliable estimates of the true mean when the sample size is large.

At small  𝑛, the running averages vary greatly between runs.

As  𝑛 grows, all lines approach the true mean and fluctuations shrink.

The histogram confirms that large-sample averages concentrate around the expected value.

This behavior is the essence of the Law of Large Numbers: randomness cancels out over many repetitions.



## Conclusion

The simulation and visualizations show that with many independent trials:

 - Random results become predictable in their average.
 - The sample mean stabilizes near the true probability 𝑝.
 - Variability decreases as n increases.

Thus, the Law of Large Numbers guarantees that empirical averages are trustworthy estimates of theoretical expectations in the long run.
