[Law of Large Numbers (LLN)](https://www.probabilitycourse.com/chapter7/7_1_1_law_of_large_numbers.php) asserts that as a random experiment is repeated numerous times, the average of the outcomes converges toward the expected value.

```For repeated, independent trials, the empirical mean stabilizes near the theoretical expectation as the number of trials increases.```

## Approach

Let X₁, X₂, …, Xₙ be independent and identically distributed random variables with expectation E[X].  
The sample mean:

$$
f(n) = \frac{X_1 + X_2 + \cdots + X_n}{n}
$$

According to the Law of Large Numbers:

$$
f(n) \to E[X] \quad \text{as } n \to \infty
$$

We model Xᵢ as Bernoulli trials (success = 1, failure = 0) with probability p = 0.3.

### Interpretation

- Fluctuations decrease as the number of trials increases.  
- All trajectories cluster close to expected value p = 0.3.  
- Empirical averages converge to theoretical expectation, illustrating the Law of Large Numbers.

---

## Conclusion

- Random results become predictable in their average.  
- Sample mean stabilizes near true probability p.  
- Variability decreases as n increases.
