### Counting Process Poisson

- We consider a time interval $$ T = 1 $$ during which events occur independently and uniformly in time at a constant average rate $$ \lambda $$

- To approximate this process numerically, divide the interval into $$ n $$ small subintervals of equal length
$$ \Delta t = \frac{T}{n}. $$

- In each of these subintervals, generate an event with probability $$ p = \frac{\lambda}{n}. $$

- This creates a discrete-time counting process that approximates a continuous-time process as $$ ( n \to \infty ). $$

---

### What Stochastic Process This Approximates

As $$ ( n ) $$ becomes large, the Bernoulli-counting construction converges to a **Poisson process** with rate $$ ( \lambda ) $$ : $$ \{N(t) : t \in [0,1]\}. $$

For any time \( t \):

$$
N(t) \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda t).
$$

In particular:

$$
N(1) \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda).
$$

---

### Theoretical Properties of the Limiting Poisson Process

## 1. Independent increments

For two disjoint time intervals:

$$
N(t_2) - N(t_1) \quad \text{and} \quad N(t_4) - N(t_3)
$$

are independent.

## 2. Stationary increments

Counts depend only on the interval length:

$$
N(t+h) - N(t) \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda h).
$$

## 3. Distribution of inter-arrival times

Let $$ ( T_1, T_2, \dots ) $$ be event times.  
Define inter-arrival times:

$$
X_k = T_k - T_{k-1}.
$$

Then each $$ ( X_k ) $$ is exponentially distributed:

$$
X_k \sim \mathrm{Exponential}(\lambda).
$$

with mean

$$
\mathbb{E}[X_k] = \frac{1}{\lambda}.
$$

---

### Meaning of the Rate Parameter $$ ( \lambda ) $$

The parameter $$ ( \lambda ) $$ represents the expected number of events per unit time:

$$
\mathbb{E}[N(1)] = \lambda.
$$

- Large $$ ( \lambda ) $$ → frequent events, steep sample paths  
- Small $$ ( \lambda ) $$ → rare events, flatter paths

### Summary

- Simulating a Bernoulli event in each of $$ ( n ) $$ small intervals with probability $$ ( \lambda/n ) $$ produces a discrete counting process that converges to a Poisson process with rate $$ ( \lambda ) $$. The limiting process has Poisson-distributed increments, independent increments, and exponentially distributed inter-arrival times.
