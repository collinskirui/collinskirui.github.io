[Dataset](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/data-science/what-is-dataset/) is a structured collection of related data, usually organized in rows and columns, representing information about a specific category or domain.


## Dataset
A dataset forms the foundation for many operations, techniques and models used across industries.
Example: A student dataset may include rows for each student and columns for attributes like name, age, grade, and marks.


## Distribution 
A distribution describes how the values of a variable are spread or occur.
It can be represented as a function, table, or graph showing which values are possible and how often they appear.
The probability distribution of a random variable can be characterized by parameters such as mean, standard deviation, minimum, and maximum values.

## Distributions can be classified as:
1. ***Univariate Distribution*** - Involves only one variable. It studies the frequency, pattern, or spread of that single variable in the dataset.
2. ***Bivariate Distribution*** - Involves two variables per observation. It examines how the two variables are related or whether one affects the other.
3. ***Multivariate Distribution*** -Involves three or more variables per observation. It studies complex relationships and interactions among multiple features simultaneously.

### Compute the k univariate distribution (k≤3). Optional: compute a bivariate distribution.

To analyze the dataset, I computed the univariate distribution for up to three variables (k ≤ 3). This involves examining the frequency or spread of each variable individually. For example:

``Customer: Number of purchases per customer``

``Product: Count of each product sold``

``Price (€): Frequency of each price point``


## For k = 1 → Univariate Distributions

### (i) Product Frequency
```
SELECT Product, COUNT(*) AS Frequency
FROM Sales
GROUP BY Product;
```

![k=1 Product Frequency](/assets/img/posts/2024-10-12-Datasets and Distrubtion/1-Product Frequency.png)




### (ii) Price Distribution 
```
SELECT 
  CASE
    WHEN Price < 400 THEN 'Low (<400)'
    WHEN Price BETWEEN 400 AND 800 THEN 'Medium (400-800)'
    ELSE 'High (>800)'
  END AS PriceRange,
  COUNT(*) AS Frequency
FROM Sales
GROUP BY PriceRange;
```
![k=1 Price Distribution](/assets/img/posts/2024-10-12-Datasets and Distrubtion/1-Price Distribution.png)


### (iii) Customer Frequency
```
SELECT Customer, COUNT(*) AS Frequency
FROM Sales
GROUP BY Customer;
```

![k=1 Customer Frequency](/assets/img/posts/2024-10-12-Datasets and Distrubtion/1-Customer Frequency.png)



## For k = 2 → Bivariate Distribution 
### (i)Customer × Product
```
SELECT Customer, Product, COUNT(*) AS Frequency
FROM Sales
GROUP BY Customer, Product;
```
![k=2 Customer × Product](/assets/img/posts/2024-10-12-Datasets and Distrubtion/2-Customer × Product.png)



### (ii)Product × Price Range
```
SELECT
  Product,
  CASE
    WHEN Price < 400 THEN 'Low (<400)'
    WHEN Price BETWEEN 400 AND 800 THEN 'Medium (400-800)'
    ELSE 'High (>800)'
  END AS PriceRange,
  COUNT(*) AS Frequency
FROM Sales
GROUP BY Product, PriceRange;
```
![k=2 Product × Price Range](/assets/img/posts/2024-10-12-Datasets and Distrubtion/2-Product × Price Range.png)




### For k = 3 → Multivariate Distribution
```
SELECT 
    Customer,
    Product,
    CASE
      WHEN Price < 400 THEN 'Low (<400)'
      WHEN Price BETWEEN 400 AND 800 THEN 'Medium (400-800)'
      ELSE 'High (>800)'
    END AS PriceRange,
    COUNT(*) AS Frequency
FROM Sales
GROUP BY Customer, Product, PriceRange;
```
![k=3 Multivariate](/assets/img/posts/2024-10-12-Datasets and Distrubtion/3-Multivariate.png)
