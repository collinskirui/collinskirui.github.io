# **Analogies Between the  LLN, Bernoulli Process and the Random Walk**

---

## 1. The Bernoulli Process

Both the **Law of Large Numbers (LLN)** simulation and the **Random Walk** experiment start from the same stochastic model  a sequence of **independent Bernoulli trials**:

$$
X_i =
\begin{cases}
1 & \text{with probability } p,\\[4pt]
0 & \text{with probability } q = 1 - p.
\end{cases}
$$

For \(n\) trials:

$$
K_n = \sum_{i=1}^{n} X_i
$$

This forms the **Binomial distribution**:

$$
P(K_n = k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k q^{n-k}.
$$

---

## 2. Structural Similarity — Shared Combinatorial Concept

| Concept | LLN Simulation | Random Walk |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Trial outcomes | 0 (failure), 1 (success) | −1 (down), +1 (up) |
| Random variable | $$K_n = \sum X_i$$ | $$S_n = \sum Y_i = 2K_n - n$$ |
| Distribution | $$\Pr(K_n = k) = \binom{n}{k}p^k q^{n-k}$$ | $$\Pr(S_n = s) = \binom{n}{(n+s)/2}p^{(n+s)/2}q^{(n-s)/2}$$ |
| Structure | Binomial coefficients → Pascal’s triangle | Same coefficients, re-centered |
| Limit law | LLN: $$\bar{X}_n = K_n/n \to p$$ | CLT: $$S_n/\sqrt{n}$$ normalizes |

---

## 3. Pascal’s Triangle Connection

The **binomial coefficients** $$\binom{n}{k}$$ form **Pascal’s triangle**, defined recursively as:

$$
\binom{n}{k} = \binom{n-1}{k-1} + \binom{n-1}{k}.
$$

Each row in Pascal’s triangle represents all possible outcomes for \(n\) Bernoulli trials.

---

## 4. Binomial Expansion and Normalization

$$
(p+q)^n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} p^k q^{n-k} = 1.
$$

This identity shows that the total probability of all outcomes = 1.

---

## 5. Key Difference — What Each Measures

| Property | LLN | Random Walk |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Quantity of interest | $$\bar{X}_n = K_n/n$$ | $$S_n = 2K_n - n$$ |
| Expected value | $$E[\bar{X}_n] = p$$ | $$E[S_n] = n(2p - 1)$$ |
| Limiting behavior | Converges to a constant | Fluctuates around a mean |
| Interpretation | Fraction of successes stabilizes | Particle drifts and diffuses |

---

## 6. Limiting Laws

- **Law of Large Numbers (LLN):**

$$
\frac{K_n}{n} \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} p \quad \Rightarrow \quad \frac{S_n}{n} = 2\frac{K_n}{n} - 1 \xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}} 2p - 1.
$$

- **Central Limit Theorem (CLT):**

$$
\frac{S_n - n(2p - 1)}{\sqrt{4npq}} \xrightarrow{d} N(0,1).
$$

---

## 7. Fibonacci, Catalan, and Combinatorial Families

### (a) Binomial → Pascal recursion

$$
\binom{n}{k} = \binom{n-1}{k-1} + \binom{n-1}{k}.
$$

### (b) Fibonacci recursion

$$
F_{n+1} = F_n + F_{n-1}.
$$

### (c) Catalan recursion

$$
C_n = \frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}.
$$

## 8. Unified Mathematical Relationships

| Concept | Formula | Relation |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Bernoulli trial | $$X_i\in\{0,1\}$$ | Basic random variable |
| Random walk step | $$Y_i = 2X_i - 1$$ | Reparameterization |
| Binomial probability | $$\Pr(K_n=k) = \binom{n}{k}p^k q^{n-k}$$ | Foundation of both |
| Pascal’s triangle | $$\binom{n}{k}=\binom{n-1}{k-1}+\binom{n-1}{k}$$ | Recursive path-counting |
| Binomial expansion | $$(p+q)^n = \sum \binom{n}{k}p^k q^{n-k}$$ | Probability normalization |
| LLN | $$\bar{X}_n\to p$$ | Stabilization of averages |
| Random walk | $$S_n = 2K_n - n$$ | Binomial counts as motion |
| CLT | $$\frac{S_n-n(2p-1)}{\sqrt{4npq}}\to N(0,1)$$ | Gaussian limit |
| Fibonacci | $$F_{n+1}=F_n+F_{n-1}$$ | Additive path recursion |
| Catalan | $$C_n=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}$$ | Constrained binomial paths |

---

## 9. Conclusion

- **Same origin:** Both LLN and Random Walk start from independent Bernoulli trials.  
- **Same combinatorial base:** Binomial coefficients, Pascal’s triangle, and binomial expansion encode all possible outcomes.  
- **Different focus:** LLN studies *average stability*; Random Walk studies *path fluctuations*.  
- **Extended families:** Fibonacci and Catalan sequences arise from modifying or constraining the same combinatorial process.
