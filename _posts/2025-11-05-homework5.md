When analyzing *data*, two key concepts help us understand it: *location* and *dispersion.*
- Location tells us where the data “centers” **(central tendency)**.
- Dispersion tells us how spread out the data points are.
Understanding both is essential for making sense of real-world and statistical datasets.

---

## 1. Measures of Location (Central Tendency)

Measures of location summarize a “typical” value in a dataset. The most common are:

### 1.1 Arithmetic Mean (Average)

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n x_i}{n}
$$

- **Use:** General statistics, business metrics, scientific research  
- **Advantages:** Uses all data points; easy to calculate  
- **Limitations:** Sensitive to extreme values or outliers  
- **Example:** Test scores: 70, 75, 80 → Mean = 75

---

### 1.2 Median

- Middle value in an ordered dataset  
- **Use:** Skewed distributions (e.g., incomes, house prices)  
- **Advantages:** Not affected by outliers  
- **Limitations:** Does not reflect all data values  
- **Example:** Data: 10, 20, 30, 100 → Median = 25

---

### 1.3 Mode

- Most frequently occurring value  
- **Use:** Categorical or qualitative data  
- **Advantages:** Works for numbers and categories  
- **Limitations:** Can be multiple or nonexistent  
- **Example:** Data: 3, 5, 3, 7 → Mode = 3

---

## 2. Measures of Dispersion (Spread)

Measures of dispersion indicate the variability of data points around the center.

### 2.1 Range

$$
\text{Range} = X_{\text{max}} - X_{\text{min}}
$$

- **Use:** Quick estimate of spread  
- **Advantages:** Simple to compute  
- **Limitations:** Sensitive to extremes

---

### 2.2 Variance

$$
\sigma^2 = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \bar{x})^2}{n}
$$

- **Use:** Risk analysis, modeling, inferential statistics  
- **Advantages:** Uses all data; essential for many statistical techniques  
- **Limitations:** Units are squared; sensitive to outliers

---

### 2.3 Standard Deviation (SD)

$$
\sigma = \sqrt{\text{Variance}}
$$

- **Use:** Measures average deviation from the mean; widely applied in science, business, and finance  
- **Advantages:** Same units as data; interpretable  
- **Limitations:** Sensitive to extreme values  
- **Example:** Scores: 5, 7, 9 → Mean = 7, SD ≈ 1.63

---

### 2.4 Interquartile Range (IQR)

$$
\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1
$$

- **Use:** Detects outliers; useful for skewed data  
- **Advantages:** Focuses on the middle 50%; robust to extreme values  
- **Limitations:** Ignores data outside middle 50%  
- **Example:** Data: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 → Q1 = 3, Q3 = 7 → IQR = 4

---

## 3. Practical Comparison Table

| Measure | Best For | Advantages | Limitations |
|---------|----------|-----------|-------------|
| Mean | Symmetric numeric data | Uses all data points; easy for modeling | Sensitive to outliers |
| Median | Skewed data | Resistant to extreme values | Ignores all other data points |
| Mode | Categorical / repeated values | Highlights common occurrence | Can be non-unique |
| Range | Quick variability check | Simple | Extremely sensitive to outliers |
| Variance | Risk and statistical modeling | Uses all data; fundamental for statistics | Squared units; sensitive to outliers |
| SD | Spread around mean | Interpretable units | Sensitive to outliers |
| IQR | Skewed distributions / outlier-resistant | Focus on central 50% | Ignores extremes |

---

## 4. Key Takeaways

- **Measures of location** tell us the “typical” value  
- **Measures of dispersion** show how variable the data are  
- Using both together provides a **complete understanding of a dataset**, crucial for **research, business decisions and everyday analysis**
